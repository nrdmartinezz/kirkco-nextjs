Advanced Filament Winding Systems for High-Strength Composite Production





Filament winding is a composite manufacturing process in which continuous fiber reinforcements such as glass, carbon, or aramid are impregnated with resin and wound under tension over a rotating mandrel to create hollow or cylindrical composite parts. The precise control of fiber placement and resin saturation allows the production of components with high structural performance, excellent dimensional control, and minimal waste.

How the Filament Winding Process Works





In the filament winding process, continuous fibers (rovings or tows) are impregnated with resin, either by drawing them through a resin bath or using a metered injection system, before being wound around a rotating mandrel. The winding carriage moves longitudinally while the mandrel spins, placing fibers at defined angles. Once the required build-up thickness is achieved, the part is cured and removed from the mandrel.









Filament Winding Application





Filament winding is widely used in industries that demand lightweight, structurally strong composite components, including:

Pressure vessels and tanks

Pipes and tubes

Aerospace structures

Automotive and industrial components

Renewable energy parts, such as wind turbine structures










Key Advantages of Filament Winding





Filament-wound composites offer several performance benefits compared to many traditional materials and molding methods:

Exceptional Strength-to-Weight Ratio:
 Continuous fibers aligned to load paths deliver superior mechanical performance while minimizing weight.

Precise Fiber Orientation &amp; Customization:
 Automated winding patterns enable tailored fiber directions that optimize stiffness, strength, and fatigue resistance.

High Repeatability &amp; Automation:
 The process can be highly automated, reducing labor costs and improving production consistency.

Design Flexibility:
 Variable winding patterns and resin systems allow manufacturers to address complex performance requirements.

These advantages make filament winding ideal for high-performance composite parts where strength, durability, and repeatable quality are critical.
